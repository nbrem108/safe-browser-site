The whole idea

You write the list.

Most filters try to guess what's bad and block it. Tiny Internet works the other way round — nothing opens unless you put it on the list. A short list you can read in one glance is easier to trust than a promise about everything else.

🚫 Anything not on your list simply doesn't open — and your child gets a friendly note saying so.

What it does

Three things, done properly.

🔗

Links can't wander

Tapping a link that leads off your list stops right there. Pop-ups and redirects stop too, so the usual ways out are closed.

🧹

Ads and trackers removed

Advertising and tracking scripts are blocked before they load. Pages arrive quieter, with less pulling at your child's attention.

🌿

A calmer page

Calm and Reader views strip out autoplay video, animation and clutter when a site is busier than you'd like.

Privacy

What we don't collect.

This is the part worth reading twice, because it's usually where children's software disappoints.

  • ✕No account. No email address. Nothing to sign up for.
  • ✕No analytics, no usage tracking, no crash reporting.
  • ✕Your child's browsing is never sent to an AI service.
  • ✕Nothing about your child reaches us. There's no server to reach.

For honesty's sake

What does leave the computer.

Three things, and we’d rather say so plainly than let you find out later.

  • →The sites your child visits are requested from those sites, the way any browser works.
  • →An ad and tracker blocklist is downloaded when the app starts, so blocking stays current.
  • →On Windows, the app asks us once at startup whether a newer version exists. That request carries your IP address and the version you are running, and nothing about your child.

The history stays on your computer. Records of what your child visited are kept locally, shown only to you, and deleted after thirty days.

For you, not for them

The parent's side.

🔑

One password

You set it the first time you open the app. Settings, the site list and unlocking a paused session all need it. No default password, and no way to switch it off.

📋

What they actually visited

A straightforward record of sites opened and sites blocked, with the reason. No dashboard to decipher.

🙋

Requests, not arguments

When your child wants a site that isn't on the list, it comes to you as a request to allow for that visit or decline. During the beta the list itself is one we curate and publish — you can read every entry, and permanently adding your own is coming.

Getting it

Still an early beta.

Tiny Internet is being tested with a small group of families before it goes out widely. It's free during the beta, and everyone who joins the beta keeps it free for good.

You will see a warning the first time you open it. During the beta the app isn't yet registered with Microsoft and Apple, so both will ask whether you're sure. That registration is coming before the wider release.

On Windows: choose More info → Run anyway.

On a Mac: open it once and let it be refused, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway. Older advice to right-click and choose Open no longer works — Apple removed that shortcut in macOS Sequoia.

What it sends. With the settings it ships with, nothing about your child leaves your computer — no account, no analytics, no crash reports. Three things do go out and none of them is about your child; the full list is here, written from the source code.

If something goes wrong. Email support@tinyinternet.app. The parent dashboard has a Show details for support button that reads off the version and the computer it's running on for you to paste in — you see all of it before you send anything. The app keeps no error log and has no crash reporting, so nothing reaches us unless you send it.

Updates. On Windows the app updates itself — it downloads new versions quietly in the background and they apply the next time it's closed and reopened. On a Mac you'll need to download it again when we tell you there's a new one; automatic updates there need the Apple registration mentioned above.

Worth knowing before you install. Tiny Internet is an app, not a setting on the computer. It controls what happens inside its own window — a child who can open a different browser can still do that. It works best alongside the screen-time controls already built into Windows and macOS, not instead of them.
